Two stories.

New York · October 2025

The convention that shouldn't have been possible — and the offhand comment that became a product.

Our manufacturing co-founder was about to fly to China. Before he left, we needed real user feedback to guide production decisions. The vendor slots for the event I needed were closed. I reached out anyway — explained who we were, what we were building, and why our presence would matter to their community. We got added to a sold-out lineup with less than a week to prepare.

I ran one-on-one interviews in the coffee area before the doors opened each day — no booth, no pressure, just real conversations. I bought the registration volunteer a coffee. She got us a private room in the building. Our co-founder flew in from the factory and did deep product sessions there. On the show floor, in the lobby, in the queue — I kept talking to people.

In one of those conversations, a user mentioned she wanted her AI companion to wear matching outfits. I didn't let it pass. I kept hearing versions of it. I brought it back to the Discord as a poll — the signal held. I pushed it formally to the team. The co-founders — Stanford engineers — thought it was too informal, not technical enough for an AI startup. I showed them the data. Matching Outfit launched.

500 new community members from this event alone — highest purchase-intent cohort we tracked
70%+ of Batch 2 buyers added Matching Outfit at $100+ · every restock since has sold out in under 5 min

San Francisco · December 2025

30 videos. 15 days. A power outage on Christmas Eve. 5 minutes to sell out.

December 9th. Pre-sale date: December 24th. This was Funding Season — investors were watching, and the result would directly affect the next round. It couldn't just go well. It needed to be a statement.

I built a complete content calendar before anything else. 30 videos in 15 days — two a day — each with a different CTA calibrated to how many days remained before launch. I coordinated daily with our designer on countdown visuals, synced with our co-founder in China for the latest hardware footage from the factory floor, and — while running all of that — noticed no one had built the Sold Out page. I shipped it before anyone asked.

December 24th. San Francisco, 5pm PST. The office lost power that afternoon. We drove around in the rain looking for a café with working wifi. Found one, ordered coffee, opened the backend, and watched. Five minutes after the sale opened, all 100 units were gone. Discord exploded — thousands of messages in minutes. People saying what they bought, what outfit they'd ordered for it, where they'd put it in their apartment. The kind of reaction that makes the 15 days feel short.

5 min to sell 100 units on Dec 24 · investors saw the data the same night
30 videos in 15 days · scripted, shot, edited, published, and tracked — solo
